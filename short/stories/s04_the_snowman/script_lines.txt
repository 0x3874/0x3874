The snowman grew every night.
On Monday, it came up to Noah's knees.
On Tuesday, his waist.
On Wednesday, his shoulders.
By Friday, it was taller than the porch.
Nobody in town knew why.
Noah checked for footprints.
There were none.
He asked the neighbors.
They only shrugged.
So he set an alarm for midnight.
He tiptoed to the window.
Snow fell in thick, soft flakes.
A small figure in a red scarf rolled a giant ball across the yard.
It was his little sister.
She was whispering to the snowman.
"Just a little bigger," she said.
"So Noah won't be sad when it melts."
Noah's eyes filled with tears.
He went outside in his socks.
"It's okay," he said.
"I'm not sad."
She grinned and handed him a scoop of snow.
They built it taller together.
At dawn, the snowman wore a tiny shirt and a goat's horns.
Thank you.
