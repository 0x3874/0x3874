Never count the stairs at night.
In this town, that rule is older than the houses.
Nobody explains why.
Noah had always counted twelve.
Twelve steps from the hall to his bedroom.
Twelve steps back.
One night, he came home late.
The house was dark.
His little sister was asleep upstairs.
He climbed slowly.
He counted without meaning to.
One, two, three.
Four, five, six.
Seven, eight, nine.
Ten, eleven, twelve.
He stopped.
There was one more step.
It had never been there.
Noah looked down at the dark hallway.
He looked back up.
The thirteenth step was warm.
It was breathing.
Slowly, it shifted.
Two long toes curled around the edge.
Noah understood the rule.
It was never a step.
It was the foot of something lying across the whole house.
Noah did not move.
The foot began to lift.
And the whole house breathed in.
Fourteen.
